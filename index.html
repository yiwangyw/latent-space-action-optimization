Latent-Space Action Optimization under Implicit Constraints for Robotic Manipulation

Yi Wang1, Ko Ayusawa2, Gentiane Venture1, Eiichi Yoshida3
1The University of Tokyo 2AIST 3Tokyo University of Science
Paper Video BibTeX

Abstract

Robot manipulation tasks can often be formulated as constrained optimization problems, where feasibility constraints arise from dynamics, object contacts, and kinematic requirements. Effectively formulating these implicit and hybrid constraints for real-world tasks, however, remains challenging.

We propose a data-driven reparameterization approach that enables optimization-based motion planning without explicit constraint specification. Given feasible demonstrations, we train a Transformer-based conditional variational autoencoder whose decoder maps latent vectors to feasible actions consistent with the current visual observation. Planning is then performed by optimizing the latent variable for an additional user-specified objective using standard gradient-based optimizers. A prior-aware penalty keeps optimization consistent with the learned latent distribution and restricts the search to data-driven action constraints.

Experiments on a real-world, contact-rich block assembly task show that the learned decoder preserves feasibility at rates of at least 97% across all phases. Latent-space optimization also improves motion quality, reducing insertion-phase jerk by 14.61% and acceleration by 10.84% compared with unoptimized latent sampling.

Approach

Learn feasibility, then optimize intent

01

Implicit constraints

A Transformer-CVAE represents phase-dependent feasibility directly from expert demonstrations, without explicit geometric or contact models.

02

Latent planning

The decoder maps a 32-dimensional latent variable to a 60-step, 6-DoF action horizon, replacing 360 raw action variables with a compact search space.

03

Robust execution

Receding-horizon control, visual conditioning, warm starts, and a prior-aware penalty preserve feasibility under disturbances and test-time optimization.

Real-world evaluation

Contact-rich block assembly

The robot aligns two arch-shaped blocks to form a circle while keeping two side blocks stable. Each phase imposes distinct collision, pose, contact, and motion constraints.

The study uses a 6-DoF LeRobot SO101 arm, a two-finger gripper, two RGB cameras, and 84 feasible demonstrations collected at 30 Hz.
